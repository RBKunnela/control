ADR-0079: A signed plane admits a child only if it saw it before its parent's close

Status: accepted Date: 2026-11-09

Amends ADR-0034 on a child of a closed parent, and ADR-0076 with a second marker in state/. Builds on ADR-0078.

Context

Under approvals.authority: signed the plane judges a run by its own record and by the close it carried into state/ (ADR-0076). runs open --key --parent P refuses a closed parent only by reading records/, which the commands read without state/. After the plane carried P's close, whoever may write records/ can put P's open record back; an operator's command then signs a child of P, and the plane admits it, since it never looks at the parent. ADR-0034 keeps a child running when its parent closes, so the plane has to tell a child opened before the close from one opened after it.

Decision

  • Under signed, at every resolve of a run with a parent, after its own record is taken and is not refused as replayed, the plane reads state/<id>.seen. Where that marker exists, no ancestor is read.
  • Where it does not, the plane walks up from the parent. At each ancestor whose close is carried (state/<ancestor>.closed stands, or the plane holds the close because state/ did not take it) the call is refused as closed with runs.ErrParentClosed, naming that ancestor, counted under runs.signed as refused_parent_closed, apart from a replay. The walk stops at the root or at an ancestor with a .seen, whose descendants keep ADR-0034; between the two it reads and judges each ancestor's record as any record, so a child refused under a put-back parent cannot vouch for a grandchild. A record that does not count, or more than 32 ancestors with no .seen, refuses the call as unreadable.
  • Otherwise the plane creates an empty state/<id>.seen, forced to disk, as the last step before the call is admitted, after the root's state. A write that fails refuses the call and removes the marker it created, forcing the removal to disk; a marker that is not a regular file of the plane's alone refuses it as unreadable. Within one plane the calls of a child take these steps one at a time, from the read of its .seen on, so none relies on a marker another has not finished.
  • .closed keeps its empty format. Nothing changes off signed.
  • A restore closes every run (ADR-0078), so it neither copies nor needs a .seen: it accepts one in state/, the live one or the backup's, and counts none as a run.

Security / compatibility impact

The plane decides from what it saw itself, never from an operator's signed time. Breaking under signed: a child a 0.16 plane admitted has no .seen, so once its parent's close is carried it is refused; the operator opens it again. ADR-0034's "closing a parent closes none of its children" now holds for the children the plane admitted before it carried the close. A child signed before the close whose first call comes after the carry is refused all the same.

Limits: a close the plane never took leaves no marker, so a child of a parent whose close the plane has not carried yet is admitted and marked. A stdio plane resolves its token at the start before it carries the closes it finds there, so its own child is judged before those closes are carried. The plane's own account can delete a .closed or create a .seen (ADR-0076). A child whose first call races the carry of its parent's close may be admitted: it was signed before the carry, and no lock is taken for it.

Alternatives considered

  • The signed times, closed_at <= opened_at: two operators' clocks skew, and a child put back after the close passes by the skew.
  • The signed times with a margin: still a clock, and more code.
  • The commands read state/: a new operator read of the plane's state, which ADR-0076 keeps from them.
  • A stated limit: leaves a closed parent's children open to whoever writes records/.

Consequences

state/ holds one empty file per child the plane admitted, which a backup copies. An operator reopens a child refused after an upgrade. A crash between the marker's creation and the end of the admission or of its removal, or a removal that fails, leaves a .seen for a refused call, and that child then runs after its parent's close is carried. Planes that share one runs directory do not wait for each other, so one may rely on a marker another plane has not finished.

Validation

internal/runs tests the put-back parent with a child signed by the operator's path, carried at a call and at the start, refused at every resolve, and a grandchild signed under that child; a grandchild under a child's carried close and under an admitted child of a closed root; an ancestor's close or record the plane cannot trust; the walk's bound; a child admitted before the carry across a restart; a child signed before the close but first called after it; a held close; a first admission whose root's state, .seen or its directory fails to reach the disk, leaving no marker, a removal forced to disk, a marker found kept by a failing call, a removal that fails named in the error, a second call held while a first forces its marker, before and after the carry, and a marker that is no regular file of the plane's; an unsigned layout; and a restore over .seen. internal/recovery restores a backup holding one.

Amendment: the walk carries the close it reads

The walk reads the record of each ancestor below the root that the plane never admitted, and took one as a link whether it was open or closed. Where it reads that record closed, it now carries the close into state/ and refuses the call as closed, naming the ancestor, with no .seen made. A first admission also reads the root's state before it makes the .seen, so a call refused for a lost root state leaves no marker.

This makes the Limits paragraph above partly false: a child whose uncarried close sits on such an ancestor is refused at its first call. What still holds: the walk reads neither the root's record nor that of an ancestor with a .seen, so a child whose parent is the root or a run the plane admitted, closed but not yet carried, is admitted and marked, and keeps running once that close is carried, as ADR-0034 keeps for an admitted child. internal/runs tests a grandchild's first call under a closed parent the plane never admitted, and a first call beside a lost root state.
