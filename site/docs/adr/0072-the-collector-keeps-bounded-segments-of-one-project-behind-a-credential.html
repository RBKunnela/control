ADR-0072: The collector keeps bounded segments of one project, behind a credential

Status: accepted Date: 2026-10-30

Amends ADR-0020 on what collect writes and whom it takes it from, and ADR-0035 on what a cursor names in a log of segments. Builds on ADR-0014 and ADR-0066.

Amends ADR-0066 properties 1, 3 and 6 for RecordLog until it has a second kind.

Context

collect appended everything it received to one file that never rotated, and its index of event ids grew with it. It took a request from anything that could reach its loopback port, so any local account could write lines into a plane's trail, or fill the disk. A file that only grows has no bound to fail closed on, and no way to drop old evidence that leaves a record of what was dropped.

Decision

Evidence storage is a port, RecordLog, declared in internal/segmentlog: append whole lines, seal the live segment, prune sealed segments through one number, locate a segment by its key, and read the manifest. A key is the SHA-256 of the segment's number and its first line's digest; numbers are never given twice, so no two segments share one, and a segment keeps its key when it is sealed. A located segment is Live, Sealed or Pruned, with what the log knows of it; the zero answer is Unknown, and so is a key no segment the manifest names has. The kind is closed, and its default is segments. No remote kind exists yet.

RecordLog is a port in part, and says which part. Until a second kind exists it falls short of ADR-0066 in three ways. Its interface is declared beside its one kind, in internal/segmentlog, not by its consumer internal/trailfile. The kind is fixed in code: no configuration key selects it, so none can name an unknown one. And it has no /metrics or doctor key, because the collector has neither; trail segments prints its counts. Writes and prunes go through the port. Reads do not: export, cursors and trail segments read the default kind's files through its own read-only view, so a second kind brings its reader with it. The change that adds a second kind, or a remote one, moves the interface to its consumer, adds a read interface and a kind selector, and gives the port its bounds and diagnostics.

The default kind is a directory of segments and one manifest, segments.json, format guardana.control.segments version 0.1. The manifest names the tenant and the project the log is bound to, and each sealed or pruned segment by number, with its lines, bytes, the SHA-256 of its first and last line and when it was sealed and pruned. It is read only in the one spelling its writer gives it; any other version is refused. One writer holds the directory under a lock file, and waits up to a quarter of a second for it, so a reader's probe of the lock does not stop a writer starting. The directory is refused with any group or other access. An open reads every segment not pruned whole, and refuses one that does not hold what the manifest names it with, a segment the manifest does not name, and any name no writer leaves. The live segment is opened by name through the directory the writer judged and locked.

The manifest names at most 10,000 segments. A seal at that bound drops the oldest tombstone and counts it in dropped, so pruned segments never fill the manifest; 10,000 sealed segments refuse the next seal until an operator prunes. A cursor of a dropped segment names a key nothing has, and is refused as another file, never read as another position.

A seal names the segment in the manifest, then renames the live file; a prune writes the tombstone, then unlinks. A crash between the steps is completed at the next open: a live file that is the segment the manifest names sealed is renamed, and a file at a pruned segment's name is unlinked when it is the one the tombstone names. Anything else is refused.

The bound refuses and nothing prunes on its own. collect --max-bytes bounds the bytes of the segments not pruned; an append that would pass it is answered 503, the plane's spool fills, and a material call is blocked with EVIDENCE_UNAVAILABLE as ADR-0014 says. A live segment holding a line is sealed before an append would take it past --segment-bytes, and before the bound on the log is asked. collect refuses a segment bound below the largest request a plane sends, and a log bound below twice it, so a full log always holds a sealed segment to prune. guardana-gateway prune --through <n> <dir> is the operator's act, refused while a collector holds the directory.

The collector is bound to one tenant and project and requires a credential. collect --tenant --project --credential-file is required. The receiver answers 401, before it reads the body, a request whose one Authorization header is not Bearer and the credential, compared as SHA-256 digests in constant time; the plane sends it through export.headers. The first refusal is logged, then a count at most once a minute. An append holding an event of another tenant or project is refused whole as 400, which the plane quarantines. A body the receiver could not read to its end, cut by a reset connection or a read timeout, is answered 503 and sent again; one that never arrives in time fills the spool and blocks material calls.

The evidence export goes to 1.1, and keeps its meaning. An export of a directory, trail export <dir>, reads one segment. Its header's source and its cursors carry the segment's key, and its header's pruned_before counts the segments before it the export cannot read, pruned or their tombstones dropped; a single file's export carries 0. The member is additive: a reader of 1.0 takes a higher minor, and a 1.1 reader reads a 1.0 header as 0, since no released build wrote a directory export. A strict 1.0 reader, as coverage and examples/evidence-report of 0.14.0 are, refuses a 1.1 export on its new member: it fails loudly and never misreads. A coverage join refuses an export as whole while pruned_before is above 0, so a window a pruned segment held part of is never taken as checked. With no cursor it is the first segment not pruned. A file of the directory exported by its path is refused, since it would count no segment pruned before it. end_reached is the end of the log as the export found it: at the end of a sealed segment that a later one with a line follows it is false, and the export after that next_cursor reads the next segment from its first line. So a reader that follows next_cursor until end_reached reads every retained line once, and a coverage join takes an export as whole only when it reached the log's end. A cursor of a pruned segment is refused as expired, exit 2. A segment opened is read from its descriptor; a live segment opened after a seal the view did not see is refused as moved and the export starts again, so a seal or a prune while it is read changes nothing read. trail <dir> reads every segment not pruned, and trail segments <dir> prints the manifest, its counts and each segment's key.

Security / compatibility impact

collect --out is gone, and every collect command line changes once: a plane sending no credential or another one is answered 401, retries, and blocks its material calls when its spool is full, as with a collector that is down. A local account without the credential writes nothing to a trail and cannot fill its bound. One that can read the credential file, or the plane's configuration, can; both are the operator's to keep.

What this does not do: the plane does not authenticate the collector. While collect is down or restarting, any local account that binds its loopback port first receives the plane's next retry, the bearer token in plaintext among it, and can then write to the trail and fill its bound once the real collector is back. The fix, the collector authenticated to the plane on a unix socket with a peer-credential check, is planned for 0.16.

The dedup index covers the segments not pruned only: an event sent again after its segment was pruned is written again. A trail whose first events were pruned fails the chain check in trail <dir>, which sees only its rest. A trail file of the old shape is still read, and exported as before but for the header's version and pruned_before, 0.

Alternatives considered

  • Pruning the oldest sealed segment at the bound: drops evidence nobody chose to drop, and lets anyone who can fill the bound erase what came before.
  • Splitting appends across segments to keep each under its bound exactly: a request's lines would land in two segments, and a cursor would name half a request.
  • An unbound collect --out beside the segments: leaves the refusal at the bound, and the credential, for anyone to step around.
  • A credential compared as bytes: subtle.ConstantTimeCompare returns early on a length that differs, so the length would be learned; digests are of one length.

Consequences

An operator watches trail segments and prunes; until then the plane blocks material calls. The findings and observation logs take the same port in a later change. The collector has no /metrics or doctor of its own, so the port's counts are what trail segments prints.

Validation

  • internal/segmentlog: a crash before every step of a seal and of a prune, and again before every step of the open after it, leaves the log whole or not begun, located as what it is; the conformance cases in testdata/ports/record-log/, each with its negative control, one append past the segment bound among them; a manifest in any other spelling, and one that disagrees with its files, is refused; FuzzManifest holds the one spelling; a manifest at its bound drops a tombstone at a seal and refuses one at 10,000 sealed segments until a prune; a writer waits out a reader's probe of its lock.
  • internal/trailfile: an event of another project refuses its append; a resend across a seal and across a reopen is written once; an export cut at every record and resumed writes every event once, across seals; a reader following next_cursor until end_reached reads every line once; a cursor of a pruned segment is refused as expired; a live segment sealed between a view and its open is refused as moved; two segments beginning with one line keep their cursors apart.
  • adapters/otel: a request without the credential exactly, in every form tried, is answered 401 and reaches no sink, and a thousand refusals log one line.
  • cmd/guardana-gateway: prune removes the segment it names, says how many bytes, and is refused while a collector holds the directory; collect refuses bounds that could leave a full log with nothing sealed.
  • internal/gateway/e2e: at the trail's bound a material call is blocked and never reaches the upstream, nothing is pruned on its own, the collector back without a prune leaves it blocked, and after a prune under the same bound the spool drains and the call runs; a plane with another credential writes nothing; another project's evidence is quarantined by the plane.
